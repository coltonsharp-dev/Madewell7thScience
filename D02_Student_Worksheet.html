01 · Explain · Electron Shells

ELECTRON
SHELLS

Electrons don't randomly surround the nucleus — they fill energy shells in order. Shell 1 (closest to nucleus) fills first — max 2 electrons. Shell 2 — max 8 electrons. Shell 3 — max 8 electrons. 02 · Explain · Valence Electrons

VALENCE
ELECTRONS

The electrons in an atom's outermost shell are called valence electrons. They are the ones that interact with other atoms during chemical reactions. For main-group elements, the group number tells you how many valence electrons the atom has.

Group 1 — 1 Valence Electron
Li, Na, K all have 1 outer electron they want to give away. Highly reactive. The farther down the group, the easier it is to give that electron away.
Group 17 — 7 Valence Electrons
F, Cl, Br have 7 outer electrons — one spot left to fill. Very reactive. They will aggressively grab one more electron from any metal they encounter.
Group 18 — 8 Valence Electrons (Full)
He, Ne, Ar have a completely full outer shell. Almost never react. Nothing to give, no room to take. Full shell = chemically stable.

04 · Explain · Periodic Trends

PERIODIC
TRENDS

Properties don't just repeat — they follow predictable trends across and down the table. Two of the most important for 7th grade: reactivity and atomic size.

Reactivity Trend in Group 1 — Increases Going DOWN
As you go down Group 1 (Li → Na → K → Rb → Cs), each element has its valence electron in a shell farther from the nucleus. The farther the electron, the less the nucleus holds onto it, and the easier it is to give away. Easier to give away = more reactive.
Atomic Size Trend — Larger Going DOWN
Atoms get larger going down a group because each new period adds a complete new electron shell. More shells = more space around the nucleus = bigger atom. Going left → right across a period, atoms get slightly smaller because more protons pull the same shells inward.
