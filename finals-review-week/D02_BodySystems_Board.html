01 · Engage

THE 11 MAJOR
BODY SYSTEMS

Circulatory
Heart, blood, vessels. Pumps blood to every cell.
Respiratory
Lungs, trachea, diaphragm. Brings O₂ in, CO₂ out.
Digestive
Stomach, intestines. Breaks food into nutrients.
Nervous
Brain, spinal cord, nerves. Fast electrical signals.
Endocrine
Glands, hormones. Slow chemical messages.
Skeletal
206 bones, joints. Support, protection, blood cells.
Muscular
Skeletal, smooth, cardiac muscle. Movement.
Excretory
Kidneys, bladder. Filters waste from blood.
Integumentary
Skin, hair, nails. Protection, temperature.
Immune (Lymphatic)
White blood cells, lymph nodes. Fights pathogens.
Reproductive
Gonads, gametes. Produces offspring.
02 · Explore · Combo Move

RESPIRATORY +
CIRCULATORY

These two systems are useless without each other. The respiratory system can pull O₂ from the air and dump CO₂, but it can't deliver O₂ to your cells. The circulatory system can move blood, but it can't load O₂ into the blood by itself. Together, they make gas exchange happen.

Key term: ALVEOLI
Tiny grape-cluster sacs at the ends of your bronchial tubes. Walls are one cell thick, wrapped in capillaries. O₂ diffuses out → into red blood cells. CO₂ diffuses in → exhaled. You have ~480 million alveoli — total surface area is the size of a tennis court.
03 · Explore · Food's Journey

PATH OF
FOOD

1
Mouth — chewing (mechanical) + saliva (chemical, breaks down starch).
2
Esophagus — muscular tube that peristalsis (squeezes) food down to the stomach.
3
Stomach — strong muscles + hydrochloric acid + enzymes break food into chyme.
4
Small Intestine — most chemical digestion + nutrient absorption. Lined with villi for huge surface area. Aided by liver (bile) and pancreas (enzymes).
5
Large Intestine — absorbs water, forms feces.
6
Rectum / Anus — waste exits.
Common mistake
Most chemical digestion happens in the small intestine, not the stomach. The stomach mostly mashes and starts breaking down proteins.
04 · Explain · Two Messengers

NERVOUS vs
ENDOCRINE

FeatureNervous SystemEndocrine System
Signal typeElectrical impulsesChemical hormones
PathwayThrough neurons (nerves)Through bloodstream
SpeedMilliseconds — extremely fastSeconds to days — slow
Duration of effectBrief — lasts as long as the signalLong — minutes to years (puberty!)
ExamplesReflex jerk, blink, run from dangerGrowth, puberty, hunger, sleep cycle, stress
Master organ/glandBrainPituitary gland (in the brain too)
Both can affect the same target — your heart speeds up from nervous signals (sympathetic nerves) AND from endocrine signals (adrenaline). The two systems also talk to each other: the brain triggers the pituitary, which triggers other glands.
05 · Explore · Outer Layers

SKIN, BONES,
MUSCLES

Integumentary (Skin)
Largest organ. Layers: epidermis (outer barrier, dead cells), dermis (nerves, blood, sweat glands, hair roots), hypodermis (fat, insulation).

Jobs: keep pathogens out, regulate temperature (sweating, blood vessels dilating), sense touch.
Skeletal
206 bones in an adult. Functions: support, protect (skull/brain, ribs/lungs), movement (with muscles via tendons), blood cell production (red marrow), mineral storage (calcium).
Muscular
Three types: skeletal (voluntary, attached to bones), smooth (involuntary, in organs like stomach), cardiac (only in the heart). Connect to bones via tendons.
06 · Explore · Defense & Cleanup

EXCRETORY +
IMMUNE

Excretory (Urinary) System
Two kidneys filter ~50 gallons of blood per day, removing urea and excess water.
Urine flows: kidneys → ureters → bladder → urethra → out.

Other "excretory" organs: lungs (CO₂), skin (sweat), large intestine (solid waste).
Immune (Lymphatic) System
White blood cells, antibodies, lymph nodes, spleen, thymus.
Recognizes invaders (bacteria, viruses) and destroys them. Vaccines train the immune system to recognize specific pathogens before you get sick.
07 · Big Picture

NO SYSTEM
WORKS ALONE

Pick almost any everyday action — running, eating, even reading this slide — and you'll find at least 4 body systems cooperating.
ActionSystems Involved
RunningMuscular (legs) + Skeletal (bones/joints) + Nervous (brain signals) + Circulatory (delivers O₂) + Respiratory (breathes harder)
Eating dinnerDigestive (breaks food down) + Circulatory (carries nutrients) + Endocrine (insulin manages glucose) + Excretory (gets rid of waste)
Catching a coldImmune (fights virus) + Respiratory (where virus hits) + Integumentary (extra skin oils, sweat) + Nervous (you feel achy)
Going through pubertyEndocrine (hormones) + Reproductive (matures) + Skeletal (growth spurts) + Integumentary (skin changes)
08 · Vocabulary Lock-In

WORDS YOU
MUST KNOW

Capillary
Smallest blood vessel — one cell thick. Where O₂, CO₂, nutrients, and waste pass between blood and cells.
Alveolus (alveoli, plural)
Tiny sac at the end of the airway in the lungs where gas exchange happens.
Hemoglobin
Iron-rich protein in red blood cells that grabs O₂ at the lungs and releases it at body cells.
Peristalsis
Wave-like muscle contractions that push food through the digestive tract.
Villi
Tiny finger-like projections lining the small intestine; massively increase surface area for absorption.
Pituitary Gland
"Master gland" of the endocrine system; controls many other glands and triggers growth, puberty, etc.
