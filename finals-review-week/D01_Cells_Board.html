01 · Engage

THE THREE PARTS
OF CELL THEORY

Part 1
All living things are made of one or more cells. From a single bacterium to a 30-meter blue whale — every organism is built of cells.
Part 2
The cell is the basic unit of structure and function. Cells are the smallest things that can carry out the activities of life — eat, grow, respond, reproduce.
Part 3
All cells come from other cells. Cells reproduce by dividing — life never appears spontaneously from non-living matter.
Key history: Robert Hooke coined the word "cell" in 1665 looking at cork through an early microscope. The boxes he saw reminded him of small monks' rooms — cellulae.

03 · Explain · Part 1

ANIMAL CELL
TOUR

1
Nucleus — control center; stores DNA and gives instructions for everything the cell does.
2
Mitochondrion — powerhouse; runs cellular respiration to make ATP from glucose.
3
Ribosome — protein factory; reads mRNA from the nucleus and builds proteins.
4
Endoplasmic Reticulum — packaging and transport network for proteins and lipids.
5
Vacuole — storage sac; small in animal cells, holds water and waste.
6
Cell Membrane — selectively permeable boundary; controls what comes in and out.
03 · Explain · Part 2

PLANT CELL
TOUR

1
Cell Wall (plant only) — rigid layer of cellulose outside the membrane; gives the plant its shape and lets it stand without bones.
2
Chloroplast (plant only) — contains chlorophyll; runs photosynthesis: CO₂ + H₂O + light → glucose + O₂.
3
Large Central Vacuole (plant only) — stores water; pushes out against the wall to keep the plant firm. Wilting = vacuoles emptied.
4
Nucleus, mitochondria, ribosomes, ER, cell membrane — all present in plants too. Plants also do cellular respiration to use the glucose they make.
04 · Explain · Energy In

PHOTOSYNTHESIS
CO₂ + H₂O + LIGHT → GLUCOSE + O₂

What's happening
Plants capture sunlight with chlorophyll in their chloroplasts. They pull CO₂ from the air through tiny leaf pores (stomata) and H₂O from their roots, then use the light energy to assemble these atoms into glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆), releasing oxygen as a byproduct.
Why it matters
Photosynthesis is the entry point for almost all energy in Earth's food webs. Animals can't do it — they get glucose by eating plants (or eating things that ate plants). The O₂ in our atmosphere is essentially exhaust from billions of years of plants and algae photosynthesizing.
Equation to memorize
6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂
04 · Explain · Energy Out

CELLULAR RESPIRATION
GLUCOSE + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O + ATP

What's happening
In the mitochondria, the cell breaks glucose apart and combines its atoms with oxygen. This releases stored chemical energy, which the cell captures in molecules called ATP. The leftover atoms come out as CO₂ and water — the same things you exhale.
Where it happens
Inside every cell, both plant and animal. Plants run photosynthesis AND respiration; animals only run respiration (we just eat the glucose). Mitochondria are densely packed in cells that need lots of energy: muscle, heart, neurons.
Equation to memorize
C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂ → 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + ATP
05 · The Big Picture

EXACT
OPPOSITES

Notice how the two equations are mirror images. The reactants of one are the products of the other. This is why plants and animals depend on each other: we breathe out the CO₂ they need; they release the O₂ we need.
