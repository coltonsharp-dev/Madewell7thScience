01 · Explain · Part 1

WHY ATOMS
BOND

Atoms bond because of one driving force: stability. Noble gases (Group 18) have full outer shells — 8 valence electrons — and they almost never react. Every other element is trying to reach that same full-shell state. Atoms bond with other atoms to either gain, lose, or share electrons until their outer shell is full. This is called the octet rule — most atoms want 8 valence electrons.
Metals (Group 1–2)
Have 1 or 2 valence electrons — it is easier to give them away than to gain 6 or 7 more. They become positively charged ions (cations). Stability through loss.
Nonmetals (Group 15–17)
Have 5, 6, or 7 valence electrons — close to full. Easier to gain 1, 2, or 3 electrons than to lose many. They become negatively charged ions (anions). Stability through gain.
Nonmetals + Nonmetals
When neither atom can easily take from the other, they share electrons between them. Both atoms count the shared electrons toward their octet. Stability through sharing.

03 · Explain · Part 3

COVALENT
BONDING

Covalent bonding happens between two nonmetals. Instead of transferring electrons, they share one or more pairs of electrons. Both atoms count the shared pair toward their octet. The shared electrons create a bond that holds the two atoms together.
IONIC
Metal + Nonmetal · Electron Transfer
→
Metal GIVES electrons to nonmetal
⊕⊖
Creates ions — charged particles (cation + anion)
🧂
Usually forms crystals or salts at room temperature (NaCl, MgO)
💧
Conducts electricity when dissolved in water or melted
🔥
High melting points — strong ionic attraction holds the crystal together
COVALENT
Nonmetal + Nonmetal · Electron Sharing
⇄
Both atoms SHARE one or more electron pairs
○○
Creates molecules — neutral particles with shared electrons
💧
Often forms liquids or gases at room temperature (H₂O, CO₂, O₂)
⚡
Does not conduct electricity — no free-moving ions
❄
Lower melting points — molecules separate more easily than ionic crystals

04 · Explain · Ion Charges

ION
CHARGES

When an atom gains or loses electrons it becomes an ion. Group number predicts charge for main-group elements. Metals lose electrons → positive (cation). Nonmetals gain electrons → negative (anion). Opposite charges attract — that attraction IS the ionic bond.
Group 1 · Alkali
1+
1 valence e⁻ → gives 1 away → 1+ charge
Na⁺, K⁺, Li⁺
Group 2 · Alkaline
2+
2 valence e⁻ → gives 2 away → 2+ charge
Mg²⁺, Ca²⁺, Ba²⁺
Group 17 · Halogen
1−
7 valence e⁻ → gains 1 → 1− charge
Cl⁻, F⁻, Br⁻, I⁻
Group 16 · Oxygen family
2−
6 valence e⁻ → gains 2 → 2− charge
O²⁻, S²⁻
