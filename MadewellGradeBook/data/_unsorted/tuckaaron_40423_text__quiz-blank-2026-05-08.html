1
Sodium (Na) has atomic number 11 and is located in Period 3, Group 1 of the periodic table. Which statement correctly interprets sodium's position?
ANa has 3 protons, 1 electron shell, and is highly unreactiveBNa has 11 neutrons, 3 electron shells, and is a noble gasCNa has 11 protons, 3 electron shells, and 1 valence electron — making it a highly reactive metalDNa has 1 proton, 11 electron shells, and behaves like a noble gas
✓ Correct! AN 11 = 11 protons. Period 3 = 3 electron shells. Group 1 = 1 valence electron → highly reactive alkali metal.
2
The table below compares two elements. Element X is in Period 2, Group 17. Element Y is in Period 3, Group 2. Which comparison is CORRECT?
AX has more electron shells than Y and X has fewer valence electrons than YBX has fewer shells than Y (2 vs 3) but more valence electrons than Y (7 vs 2)CX and Y have the same number of shells because they are close in period numberDY has more valence electrons because it has a higher period number
✓ Correct! Period 2 = 2 shells, Period 3 = 3 shells. Group 17 = 7 valence e⁻, Group 2 = 2 valence e⁻. X has fewer shells but more valence electrons.
3
The staircase line on the periodic table marks the boundary between which two main regions?
AAlkali metals and alkaline earth metalsBTransition metals and lanthanidesCMetals and nonmetals — with metalloids running along the staircase itselfDHalogens and noble gases
✓ Correct! The staircase marks the metal/nonmetal boundary. Metalloids run along the staircase itself — they are the elements straddling both sides.
4
An atom has the electron configuration 2, 8, 7 (2 electrons in shell 1, 8 in shell 2, 7 in shell 3). Based only on this information, what can you determine about this element?
AIt is in Period 7 and Group 2 — a highly reactive alkaline earth metalBIt is in Period 2 and Group 7 — it has 2 electron shells and 7 total electronsCIt is in Period 3 (3 shells used) and Group 17 (7 valence electrons) — a halogen that wants to gain 1 electronDIt is in Period 3 and Group 8 — a transition metal with 8 inner electrons
✓ Correct! Configuration 2,8,7 uses 3 shells → Period 3. Outer shell has 7 electrons → Group 17. 7 valence electrons, one spot to fill → halogen that gains 1 electron.
5
Which of the following sequences correctly orders these Period 3 elements by increasing atomic number?
ACl → Si → Al → Mg → Na (left to right, Group 17 to Group 1)BNa → Mg → Al → Si → Cl (left to right, increasing from Group 1 to 17)CNa → Al → Mg → Cl → Si (ordered by reactivity, most to least)DMg → Na → Cl → Si → Al (ordered by atomic mass)
✓ Correct! Atomic number increases left → right across a period. Na=11, Mg=12, Al=13, Si=14, Cl=17. Reading Period 3 left to right gives that exact sequence.
6
Potassium (K) reacts explosively with water. Lithium (Li) reacts gently with water. Sodium (Na) reacts vigorously. All three are in Group 1. Placing them in order from LEAST to MOST reactive gives:
AK → Na → Li (least to most, ordered by atomic number descending)BLi → Na → K (least to most — reactivity increases going DOWN Group 1)CNa → Li → K (middle, least, most)DLi → K → Na (alphabetical, not chemical)
✓ Correct! Reactivity in Group 1 INCREASES going DOWN. Li (Period 2) is least reactive, Na (Period 3) is middle, K (Period 4) is most reactive among these three.
7
Calcium chloride (used to melt ice on roads) is formed when calcium (Ca, Group 2) reacts with chlorine (Cl, Group 17). What is the correct formula, and what type of bond holds it together?
ACaCl — ionic, because one Ca gives one electron to one ClBCaCl₂ — covalent, because both are in the same period and share electronsCCaCl₂ — ionic, because Ca²⁺ (2+) needs two Cl⁻ ions (1− each) to balance to zeroDCa₂Cl — ionic, because two calcium atoms are needed for every chlorine
✓ Correct! Ca²⁺ (2+) needs two Cl⁻ (1− each) to balance: 2+ + 1− + 1− = 0. Metal + Nonmetal = ionic bond. Formula: CaCl₂.
8
In a covalent bond, what happens to the valence electrons of the atoms involved?
AOne atom permanently transfers its electrons to the other, forming ionsBBoth atoms share one or more pairs of electrons — neither fully owns themCThe electrons are destroyed and reformed as photons of light energyDElectrons leave both atoms and flow freely through the compound as electricity
✓ Correct! In covalent bonding, electrons are SHARED between atoms. Neither atom permanently takes them. Compare to ionic = give/take = ion formation.
9
An atom loses 2 electrons when it forms an ionic bond. What ion does it become, and what kind of atom was it most likely before?
AA 2− anion — it was most likely a halogen in Group 17BA 2− anion — it was most likely a Group 16 nonmetal like oxygenCA 2+ cation — it was most likely a Group 2 metal like Mg or CaDA neutral atom — losing electrons does not change the charge
✓ Correct! Losing 2 electrons → fewer negative charges → 2+ cation. Group 2 metals (Mg, Ca) have 2 valence electrons — they lose both, giving 2+ charge.
10
A student says: "Carbon dioxide (CO₂) must be ionic because the word 'dioxide' sounds chemical and complex." What is wrong with this reasoning?
AThe student is correct — CO₂ IS ionic because carbon is a large atom that transfers electrons to oxygenBThe student is wrong only about the name — CO₂ is ionic but "dioxide" is not the reasonCCO₂ is covalent — carbon and oxygen are both nonmetals and share electrons; bond type depends on what elements are involved, not on how complex the name soundsDThe student is wrong because CO₂ has no bonds at all — carbon and oxygen are independent atoms
✓ Correct! Bond type is determined by ELEMENTS involved — nonmetal + nonmetal = covalent. C and O are both nonmetals. Name complexity has nothing to do with bond type.
