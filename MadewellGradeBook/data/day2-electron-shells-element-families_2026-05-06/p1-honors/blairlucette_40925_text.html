──── SECTION 1 — ELECTRON SHELLS ───────────

Q1 · Sodium (Na) has atomic number 11 and is in Period 3. What does Period 3 tell you about sodium's electron structure?
Answer: A · Sodium's electrons are arranged in 3 energy shells around the nucleus

Q2 · Shell 1 holds max 2 electrons and Shell 2 holds max 8. How many total electrons before needing a 3rd shell?
Answer: B · 10 — 2 in shell 1 plus 8 in shell 2

──── SECTION 2 — VALENCE ELECTRONS ─────────

Q3 · Chlorine (Cl) is in Group 17. How many valence electrons does chlorine have?
Answer: C · 7 — its group number tells you the number of valence electrons

Q4 · Li, Na, and K are all in Group 1 and behave similarly. What is the electron-based reason?
Answer: B · They all have 1 valence electron — the same outer-shell structure drives the same chemistry

──── SECTION 3 — ELEMENT FAMILIES ──────────

Q5 · Which BEST explains why alkali metals (Group 1) are so highly reactive?
Answer: C · They have 1 valence electron that is easy to give away, driving reactions with other elements

Q6 · Noble gases (Group 18) rarely react. What is the electron-based explanation?
Answer: B · Their outer shell is completely full — they have nothing to give and no room to accept electrons

──── SECTION 4 — PERIODIC TRENDS ───────────

Q7 · Moving DOWN Group 1 (Li to Cs), what happens to reactivity and why?
Answer: C · Reactivity increases because the outer electron is farther from the nucleus and easier to remove

Q8 · Why do atoms get LARGER as you move DOWN a group?
Answer: B · Each lower element has an additional full electron shell, making the overall atom larger

──── ESSENTIAL QUESTION ─────────────────────
If two elements are in the same group, what does that tell you about their electron structure and why does that make their behavior similar? (2+ specific elements with valence electron counts)

Response:
When two elements are in the same group on the periodic table, it means they have the same number of electrons in their outer shell. Because certain electrons determine how an atom reacts, elements with the same amount of them behave similarly in chemical reactions. For example, lithium and sodium are both in Group 1, so they both have only one valence electron and are super reactive. Since they both have just one electron to give away, they act the same way when they are forming compounds. This similar valence count is why they fall into the same "family" and share similar chemistry.
