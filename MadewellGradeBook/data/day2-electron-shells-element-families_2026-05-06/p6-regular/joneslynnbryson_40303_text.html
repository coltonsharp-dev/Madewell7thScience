──── SECTION 1 — ELECTRON SHELLS ───────────

Q1 · Sodium (Na) has atomic number 11 and is in Period 3. What does Period 3 tell you about sodium's electron structure?
Answer: A · Sodium's electrons are arranged in 3 energy shells around the nucleus

Q2 · Shell 1 holds max 2 electrons and Shell 2 holds max 8. How many total electrons before needing a 3rd shell?
Answer: C · 12 — each shell holds 4 more than the one before it

──── SECTION 2 — VALENCE ELECTRONS ─────────

Q3 · Chlorine (Cl) is in Group 17. How many valence electrons does chlorine have?
Answer: B · 3 — it is in Period 3, so it has 3 valence electrons

Q4 · Li, Na, and K are all in Group 1 and behave similarly. What is the electron-based reason?
Answer: B · They all have 1 valence electron — the same outer-shell structure drives the same chemistry

──── SECTION 3 — ELEMENT FAMILIES ──────────

Q5 · Which BEST explains why alkali metals (Group 1) are so highly reactive?
Answer: B · They are located on the left side of the table, where reactive elements are stored

Q6 · Noble gases (Group 18) rarely react. What is the electron-based explanation?
Answer: A · Noble gases have no electrons, so there is nothing available to react

──── SECTION 4 — PERIODIC TRENDS ───────────

Q7 · Moving DOWN Group 1 (Li to Cs), what happens to reactivity and why?
Answer: D · Reactivity increases because there are more electrons in total to react with other atoms [changed from B]

Q8 · Why do atoms get LARGER as you move DOWN a group?
Answer: D · Atoms spread out over time, so older discovered elements happen to be larger

──── ESSENTIAL QUESTION ─────────────────────
If two elements are in the same group, what does that tell you about their electron structure and why does that make their behavior similar? (2+ specific elements with valence electron counts)

Response:
Elements in the same group (vertical column) of the periodic table have the same number of valence electrons in their outermost energy shell, which dictates their similar chemical behavior. Because chemical reactions involve transferring or sharing these outer electrons, atoms with the same valence count react similarly to achieve a stable, full outer shell.
