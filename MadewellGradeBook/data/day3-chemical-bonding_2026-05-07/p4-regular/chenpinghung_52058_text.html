──── SECTION 1 — WHY ATOMS BOND ────────────

Q1 · Why do most atoms form chemical bonds with other atoms?
Answer: B · Atoms achieve a more stable structure by forming bonds to fill their outermost electron shell.

Q2 · The "octet rule" states that atoms tend to form bonds until they have ___.
Answer: A · There are 8 protons in the atomic nucleus.

──── SECTION 2 — IONIC BONDING ─────────────

Q3 · In ionic bonding, what happens to the metal atom's valence electron(s)?
Answer: D · Metal atoms gain additional valence electrons from non-metal atoms.

Q4 · After sodium (Na) gives its 1 valence electron to chlorine during ionic bonding, sodium becomes ___.
Answer: B · Na⁺ is a positively charged ion that has one less electron than proton.

──── SECTION 3 — COVALENT BONDING ──────────

Q5 · Water (H₂O) is formed from two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom — all nonmetals. What type of bond holds water together?
Answer: B ·Covalent bonds are formed when nonmetals share electrons, rather than transferring electrons.

Q6 · Which BEST describes how to tell an ionic compound apart from a covalent compound at room temperature?
Answer: C · Covalent compounds are always heavier than ionic compounds.

──── SECTION 4 — ION CHARGES & FORMULAS ────

Q7 · Magnesium (Mg) is in Group 2. What ion does it form when it reacts with a nonmetal?
Answer: B · Mg²⁺ loses 2 valence electrons and carries a 2+ charge.

Q8 · Calcium (Ca²⁺) and oxygen (O²⁻) combine. What is the correct formula?
Answer: B · In CaO, one Ca²⁺ ion is in equilibrium with one O²⁻ ion, and their charges cancel each other out to zero.

Using valence electrons, explain why Na and Cl react to form NaCl and why NaCl is stable when neither Na nor Cl alone is. Include valence electron counts, what happens during bonding, and why the compound is stable.

Response:
Sodium (Na): Located in Group 1 of the periodic table, atomic number 11. Its electron configuration is [2, 8, 1], with only one valence electron in its outermost (3rd) shell.
