──── ESSENTIAL QUESTION ─────────────────────
Using valence electrons, explain why Na and Cl react to form NaCl and why NaCl is stable when neither Na nor Cl alone is. Include valence electron counts, what happens during bonding, and why the compound is stable.

Response:
In NaCl crystals, through the aforementioned valence electron transfer, sodium ions achieve a stable 2,8 structure, and chloride ions achieve a stable 2,8,8 structure. More importantly, in the solid crystal, each ion is surrounded by ions of opposite charge (6 sodium ions are surrounded by 6 chloride ions). This three-dimensional network of ions minimizes the system's potential energy, exhibiting extremely high stability. Therefore, table salt (NaCl) can exist stably as a white solid at room temperature and pressure, no longer possessing the dangerous properties of metallic sodium or chlorine gas.
