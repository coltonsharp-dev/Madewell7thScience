01 · Explain · Why Atoms Bond

WHY ATOMS
BOND

Atoms bond because of one driving force: stability. Noble gases have full outer shells (8 valence electrons) and almost never react. Every other element is trying to reach that same stable, full-shell state. Atoms bond — giving, taking, or sharing electrons — until their outer shell is satisfied. This is called the octet rule.

Metals — Give Away Electrons → Become Positive Ions (Cations)
Group 1 has 1 valence electron — easier to give it away than gain 7 more. Group 2 has 2. They lose electrons and become positively charged.
Nonmetals — Gain Electrons → Become Negative Ions (Anions)
Group 17 has 7 valence electrons — only 1 spot away from full. They gain electrons and become negatively charged.
Nonmetal + Nonmetal — Share Electrons → Covalent Bond
When neither atom can easily take from the other, they share a pair of electrons. Both count the shared pair toward their octet.

02 · Explain · Ionic Bonding

IONIC
BONDING

Ionic bonding happens between a metal and a nonmetal. The metal gives its valence electrons to the nonmetal. Both atoms become ions — the metal becomes a positive cation, the nonmetal becomes a negative anion. Opposite charges attract and hold the compound together.

Example: Na + Cl → NaCl (Table Salt)
Sodium (Na) has 1 valence electron — it gives it to Chlorine. Na becomes Na⁺ (lost 1 electron = positive). Cl becomes Cl⁻ (gained 1 electron = negative). Na⁺ and Cl⁻ attract each other. Result: stable ionic compound.

03 · Explain · Covalent Bonding

COVALENT
BONDING

Covalent bonding happens between two nonmetals. Instead of transferring electrons, they share one or more electron pairs. Both atoms count the shared electrons toward their full outer shell. This creates a stable molecule.

Example: H₂O (Water)
Oxygen needs 2 more electrons. Hydrogen has 1 to share. Two hydrogen atoms each share 1 electron with oxygen. No transfer — sharing. Oxygen counts both shared pairs as its own, filling its outer shell.

04 · Explain · Ion Charges & Compound Formulas

ION CHARGES
& FORMULAS

Group number predicts ion charge. To write an ionic compound formula, charges must balance to zero — total positive must equal total negative.

Group 1 → 1+  ·  Group 2 → 2+
Na → Na⁺  ·  K → K⁺  ·  Mg → Mg²⁺  ·  Ca → Ca²⁺
Group 17 → 1−  ·  Group 16 → 2−
Cl → Cl⁻  ·  F → F⁻  ·  O → O²⁻  ·  S → S²⁻
