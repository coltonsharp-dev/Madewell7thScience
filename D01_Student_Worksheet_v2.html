02 · Explore

THE ORGANIZING
PROBLEM

By 1869, scientists had identified 63 elements but had no organizing system. Dmitri Mendeleev noticed that when elements were arranged by atomic mass, their properties repeated in a pattern — a periodic pattern. Modern chemists later found that atomic number — the proton count — is the true organizing principle.

Q1 · Before Mendeleev, some scientists organized elements alphabetically. What is the main problem with an alphabetical system?

Q2 · Mendeleev noticed that element properties repeated in a pattern. This repeating pattern gave us the word that still names the table today — the _____ table.

11 — sodium has 11 protons. No other element in the universe has exactly 11. This is its unique identity.
Na — from Latin natrium. Many symbols come from older names in Latin or German.
Sodium — the English name. Symbol and name often do not match (Na ≠ Sodium).
22.990 — the decimal is the tell. NOT the atomic number. Includes both proton and neutron mass, averaged across all naturally occurring isotopes.

04 · Explain · Part 2

PERIODS
& GROUPS

The table has two directions that each mean something. Periods run left → right (rows) — period number tells you the number of electron shells. Groups run top → bottom (columns) — same group means similar chemical behavior. The highlighted table below shows Period 3 in blue, Group 1 in green, and sodium (in both) in gold.

Q5 · Sodium (Na) is in Period 3. What does being in Period 3 tell you about how sodium's electrons are arranged?

Q6 · Lithium (Li), Sodium (Na), and Potassium (K) are all in Group 1. What does this predict about their chemical behavior?

05 · Explain · Part 3

THREE
REGIONS

The periodic table divides into three main regions separated by a staircase line. About 80% of elements are metals. Elements on the upper right are nonmetals. Elements along the staircase boundary are metalloids — with properties of both.
