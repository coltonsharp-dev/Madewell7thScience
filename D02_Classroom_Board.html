02 · Explain · Part 2

VALENCE
ELECTRONS

The electrons in the outermost shell are called valence electrons. They are the ones that interact with other atoms. They determine how an element reacts — and they are why elements in the same group behave the same way. Same group number = same number of valence electrons.
Group 1 · 1 valence e⁻
Li, Na, K — one lonely outer electron they desperately want to give away. Extremely reactive. That one electron explains everything about alkali metal chemistry.
Group 17 · 7 valence e⁻
F, Cl, Br — 7 outer electrons, one spot open. Desperately want to grab one more electron to fill the shell. This is why they react so aggressively with metals.
Group 18 · 8 valence e⁻
He, Ne, Ar — outer shell is completely full. Nothing to give, no room to take. This is why noble gases almost never react with anything. Full = stable = unreactive.
