02 · Explore

THE ORGANIZING
PROBLEM

The Problem · 1860s
63 known elements. No system. Properties were unpredictable. Alphabetical order told you nothing about how an element behaves or reacts.
Mendeleev's Discovery · 1869
Sort by atomic mass → properties repeat in a pattern. He called this pattern periodic. He predicted 3 missing elements before they were found.
Modern Fix · Atomic Number
Atomic mass caused ordering exceptions. Atomic number — the proton count — fixed every one. Today: atomic number = the organizing principle.

11 — sodium has 11 protons. No other element in the universe has exactly 11. This number IS the definition of sodium.
Na — from Latin natrium. Many symbols come from older names in Latin or German. Symbol and English name may not match.
Sodium — the English name. Why "Na"? Because the Latin name was used first in science.
22.990 — the decimal is the tell. NOT the atomic number. Includes proton + neutron mass, averaged across all naturally occurring isotopes.
KEY RULE: Atomic number = always whole. Atomic mass = almost never whole. That decimal tells them apart every time.

05 · Explain · Part 3

THREE
REGIONS

Metals · ~80% of all elements
Left & center block. Shiny, conduct electricity and heat, malleable. Examples: Na, Fe, Au, Ca, Cu.
Nonmetals · Upper Right
Poor conductors. Brittle as solids. Examples: O, S, Cl, N, He. Essential for all life.
Metalloids · The Staircase
Properties of both. Si and Ge conduct electricity under certain conditions. Every computer chip ever made = silicon.
